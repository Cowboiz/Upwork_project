# Product Vision

## Working Name

Project Marketplace

The final product name has not been decided yet.

---

## Vision

Build a simple marketplace where students can quickly find
designers and developers to help them complete digital projects,
while giving talented designers and developers access to
accessible project opportunities.

---

## Problem

Students frequently need help with digital projects such as:

- websites
- landing pages
- UI/UX design
- mobile apps
- prototypes
- presentations
- branding
- coding projects
- startup MVPs

However, finding the right person can be difficult.

Existing freelance platforms such as Upwork and Fiverr can feel:

- too broad
- too complex
- difficult for inexperienced clients
- highly competitive
- difficult to evaluate
- expensive for smaller student projects

Students may instead rely on friends, social media groups,
Discord communities, or personal referrals.

This process is fragmented and unreliable.

---

## Supply-Side Problem

New designers and developers also face challenges.

They often need:

- real projects
- portfolio work
- client experience
- income
- reviews
- credibility

Existing freelance platforms can be difficult for beginners
because established freelancers already have strong profiles,
reviews, and histories.

This creates a chicken-and-egg problem:

New freelancers need projects to build credibility,
but they need credibility to win projects.

---

## Target Users

### Primary Demand

Students who need help completing digital projects.

Examples:

- university students
- student founders
- student clubs
- hackathon participants
- early-stage creators

### Primary Supply

Designers and developers looking for project opportunities.

Examples:

- student developers
- junior developers
- UI/UX designers
- graphic designers
- freelance developers
- freelance designers

---

## Core Value Proposition

For students:

> Find the right designer or developer for your project
> without navigating a complex freelance marketplace.

For designers and developers:

> Find accessible project opportunities that help you
> earn money, gain experience, and build a portfolio.

---

## Product Principle

The platform should make hiring someone feel simple.

A student should be able to go from:

Idea

to

Project posted

to

Candidates

to

Match

with minimal friction.

---

## Marketplace Model

The platform connects two sides:

Student / Client

and

Designer / Developer

The initial marketplace flow is expected to be:

Student creates project
↓
Project becomes available
↓
Relevant freelancers discover project
↓
Freelancers express interest / submit proposals
↓
Student reviews candidates
↓
Student selects freelancer
↓
Project begins
↓
Project completes
↓
Both sides may review each other

The exact transaction and payment model will be defined
in the Business Model document.

---

## MVP Goal

The goal of the MVP is NOT to build a complete competitor
to Upwork or Fiverr.

The goal is to validate three assumptions:

1. Students have enough demand for this type of marketplace.
2. Designers and developers are willing to take these projects.
3. The platform can successfully create matches between them.

---

## MVP Success Metrics

Initial metrics to validate:

- number of student registrations
- number of freelancer registrations
- number of projects posted
- number of proposals submitted
- number of successful matches
- number of completed projects

The exact numeric targets will be defined after initial validation.

---

## Non-Goals

The first version does not need to become:

- a global freelance marketplace
- a complete Upwork replacement
- an enterprise hiring platform
- a full project management suite
- a large social network
- a complex AI platform

Features should only be added if they help validate
the marketplace.

---

## Product Philosophy

Build the smallest product capable of testing the marketplace.

Prioritize:

1. Marketplace liquidity
2. User trust
3. Fast matching
4. Simple UX
5. Rapid learning

over:

1. Advanced architecture
2. Large feature sets
3. Premature scaling
4. Complex automation
5. Visual perfection

---

## Open Questions

The following decisions still require validation:

- Who exactly is the first student segment?
- Can only students hire, or can anyone hire?
- Can only students freelance?
- Should freelancers apply to projects?
- Should students directly hire freelancers?
- Should pricing be fixed or negotiable?
- Should the platform handle payments in MVP?
- Should the platform charge commission?
- How will users verify identity?
- How will trust and reputation work?
